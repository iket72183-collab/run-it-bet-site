Methodology

How It Works

We do the research and the math — you make the call. Here is exactly how a pick gets made, and what each number means.

  1. 01

    Scan the whole board

    Every morning we pull every open Kalshi question we follow — daily high temperatures in nine cities (Texas first), tomorrow’s low temperatures in four Texas cities (checked separately at 9:30 AM, when Kalshi opens those questions), Texas power-grid demand, and how high Texas gas prices get this year — with live prices for every outcome. We only follow questions we have outside research for.

  2. 02

    Gather the evidence

    For each weather question, we read the National Weather Service forecast near the station Kalshi settles on: the daytime high, or tonight’s low for tomorrow’s low-temperature questions, plus our own private checks of how reliable that forecast has been at that station. For Texas grid demand, we read ERCOT’s own load forecast and today’s demand so far. For Texas gas, we read AAA’s Texas averages, the same source Kalshi settles on. A forecast is evidence, not a guarantee. Questions without outside research are not picked.

  3. 03

    Screen for the clearest calls

    Our screening model looks at every question and every side priced in our bands, picks the most defensible side from the facts, and rates how clear the call is. Only the clearest few questions move on. Most questions are cut here — that is the point.

  4. 04

    Decide

    Our AI models study the shortlist in depth and choose a side or pass. A pick must be priced 70–80¢ (Market favorite) or 18–45¢ (Long shot); coin flips and heavy favorites above 80¢ are never picked. The AI also gives its own estimate of the chance the pick wins, and a pick posts only if that estimate beats the price by at least 5 points after Kalshi’s fee, and by no more than 20. A bigger gap is too good to be true: it usually means the market knows something our research missed. Agreeing with the market is not a pick. A Market favorite pick where the AI beats the price by more than 10 points is labeled Bold call instead: the AI and the market disagree a lot, and the market is often right when that happens, so a Bold call carries extra risk. A Market favorite temperature or Texas power-demand pick also needs the forecast to sit comfortably far from the losing side, given how far the official reading has recently missed the forecast. The price always comes from Kalshi, never from the AI.

  5. 05

    Check the reasoning

    Before anything is published, the screening model checks each pick’s reason against the facts it was given. A reason that invents facts or argues for the other side is thrown out. Every pick needs outside research behind it: we don’t post picks that only follow the price. If the AI can’t run, or nothing researched is worth picking, there are no picks that day.

  6. 06

    Publish the original

    Picks lock once a day after 8 AM Central, plus one 9:30 AM check for the Texas low temperatures, and never change that day. The original call and its price are stored permanently; later prices never rewrite them.

  7. 07

    Keep it honest

    Every pick is stored permanently at the price it posted, and the market’s own settlement decides it, win or lose. A filter, not a strategy: in our own real-money test the picks showed no reliable edge over the market. In a two-month check of daily highs in four cities, 70–80¢ prices won about 75% of the time, right where the prices said, and our forecast math didn’t reliably beat them. Don’t expect these picks to.

What the numbers mean

An 80¢ price is a market-implied reference, not a verified 80% chance or an AI confidence score. A purchase at 80¢ costs $0.80 per contract and returns $1 if that side wins, before fees. A high probability does not imply a profitable price.

New paper results use the recorded buy quote; older imported calls use their original midpoint. Both exclude fees and actual fills.

Sports picks

Sports picks are our AI’s research, shared for fun. Use caution: do your own research or just enjoy the games. It’s still gambling. At the same 8 AM lock, we gather the day’s games with their sportsbook odds, plus ESPN’s injury report, team records and (for baseball) the probable starting pitchers. For basketball and hockey we also note which teams played the night before. We are not looking for the team most likely to win: the price already knows that. Our screening model keeps the few games worth a closer look, underdogs included; our AI models pick a team or pass, and every reason is fact-checked against that research before it posts. A team must sit between roughly 18% and 80% by the odds (Long shot, Close call or Market favorite), and our AI’s own estimate must beat the Kalshi price by at least 5 points after Kalshi’s fee, and by no more than 20 (a bigger gap usually means the market knows something we don’t). If nothing qualifies, there are no sports picks that day. Results come from ESPN final scores. A draw, postponement or cancellation is not a win or a loss: it is graded by what the Kalshi contract actually paid under its own rules, and shows as pending until Kalshi settles.

Sports events can end in a draw. Payouts follow the specific market’s settlement rules and may differ from a simple win or loss. A partial payout does not guarantee a profit or refund. Review the linked market rules; your purchase price, fees and any early sale affect your return.

The odds are the market’s view. Our AI’s own estimate is used only to decide whether a pick posts; we don’t show it on the cards, because in our own tracking the market’s price has been the better guide. Neither is a proven chance. Records, streaks and “against winning teams” counts on the cards come straight from ESPN, using each opponent’s current record, and a reason that gets them wrong is thrown out. If we find a mistake after a pick is published, we add a dated correction beside it and leave the original analysis as it was. We do not watch game film or use insider information.
